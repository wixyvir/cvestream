# PRD Quality Review — cvestream

## Overall verdict
This is a tight, evidence-grounded PRD of the right size for a solo/hobby tool. The problem is real and quantified, Out of Scope does real work, and several FRs are unusually precise: FR-8, FR-9, FR-13, FR-14 and FR-19 are well specified. The risk sits in the product's core loop. How a product entry matches text, which source's value wins after a merge, and whether a same-GUID "update" ever reaches Slack are all unspecified or possibly unworkable. The recall-first pass-through rules have also never been checked against the 80% volume target. None of this is broken, but it should be settled before story creation.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions, not as considerations: RSS is "the only output contract" (§ Summary), CIRCL Vulnerability-Lookup is rejected ("cvestream stays standalone", § Out of Scope), CERT-FR *avis* are excluded with a reason, and FR-19 says plainly what triggers a re-publish. The two Open Questions are real. Neither is answered in the next sentence.

The PRD does not show the cost of its main trade-off. It consistently favours recall: unknown versions "match anyway" (FR-13), unscored alerts "are never dropped" (FR-14), and matching runs on "the description text" (FR-12). It also commits to "at least 80% fewer posted items" (§ Success Metrics). The addendum's own sample shows ~13% of EUVD records with no score and ~14% with no product or "n/a". EUVD is also a far wider input than today's cvefeed "High/Critical" feed. The PRD never says which side gives way if the two collide.

### Findings
- **[high]** Recall-first pass-through rules vs. the 80% volume target are unreconciled (§ FR-12, FR-13, FR-14; § Success Metrics "Volume") — every "unknown" case passes the filters, and the input changes from cvefeed High/Critical to all of EUVD. A broad entry such as `linux` (the kernel CNA issues CVEs at high weekly volume) or a short name matched in description text could easily exceed the raw-feed volume. The PRD names no lever for this, such as a per-endpoint toggle for unknown-version or unscored items, or a CVSS floor that applies only to scored items. *Fix:* state which goal wins when they conflict, and name the per-endpoint lever the operator uses to tune volume without losing recall.
- **[medium]** The "Product aliases" open question blocks the core matching FRs (§ Open Questions; FR-11, FR-12) — the question is honest, but it is listed as a side issue while FR-12's behaviour depends on it ("PHP" vs "php-src"). *Fix:* mark it as blocking FR-11/FR-12 and record an interim v1 decision (e.g., a product entry may list aliases explicitly in configuration).

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no furniture. The Problem section cites figures ("about 190 alerts a week", "roughly 10% of them are relevant") and a dated incident (PHP2Shell) that directly motivates FR-8 and FR-12 ("titles that didn't mention PHP"). The two user roles each drive decisions: the Operator drives F5, and subscribers who "never touch the filter configuration" drive the RSS-only output. The NFRs are mostly specific to this product. Source resilience is backed by an observed EUVD breakage, and Freshness has a number. The differentiation analysis stays in the addendum ("The gap") instead of filling a template section in the body.

### Findings
- **[low]** The Footprint NFR is an adjective, not a bound (§ NFR "Footprint": "runs comfortably on one small host for a handful of endpoints") — *Fix:* give a rough envelope (e.g., ≤ 512 MB RAM, ≤ 10 endpoints) or drop it as a non-requirement.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear: relevance comes from matching structured, CNA-sourced product/version data from EU sources, not titles, and noise is cut per team without losing recall. F1–F4 follow that arc, and the success metrics include a named counter-metric (recall). The MVP is problem-solving in kind, and its scope logic fits.

The motivating incident is about timeliness as much as noise ("first heard of the issue from CERT-EU links posted by hand… The feed relayed it hours later"). The metrics measure volume, recall and duplicates, but not lead time. The recall definition also cannot see the failure mode that started the project.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The recall counter-metric cannot detect the PRD's founding failure (§ Success Metrics "Counter-metric") — recall is measured as "Any alert the team reacts to or discusses in the raw feed". PHP2Shell reached the team through links *posted by hand*, not through the raw feed. Items that exist only in the new sources (CERT-EU, KEV, EUVD-only) are also invisible to this check. *Fix:* include alerts posted by hand in the channel, and KEV additions for the team's products, in the recall reference set.
- **[medium]** The timeliness part of the thesis is not measured (§ Problem; § NFR "Freshness") — Freshness is measured from "a new source record". It does not compare against the earliest public advisory (e.g., CERT-EU, vendor), so cvestream could meet the NFR and still lag the hand-posted links. *Fix:* add an SM such as "for alerts posted by hand during the trial, the filtered feed had them no later than the hand post", or state that timeliness is out of the thesis.
- **[low]** The trial channel does not match the per-team design (§ Success Metrics: "over one month in #theme-security") — the product is "one filtered RSS feed per Slack channel" per team, but the trial runs in one shared channel. The PRD does not say which endpoint configuration (union of all stacks? one team?) is being measured. *Fix:* name the endpoint(s) and product lists used for the trial.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have a testable consequence. FR-6 (last good content), FR-8 (domain change must not duplicate), FR-9 ("never as zero or low"), FR-13 (the three "unknown" cases are listed), FR-14, FR-16 (7 days), FR-19 (the two re-publish triggers, with an example) and FR-20 are all verifiable. This is the weakest dimension relative to its importance, however: the gaps fall on matching, merging and re-publishing, which is exactly where the product's value lies.

### Findings
- **[high]** Description-text matching semantics are undefined (§ FR-12, FR-13) — "Match against … the description text" does not say whether matching is substring, token or word-boundary, case-insensitive, or vendor-qualified. `php` would match "phpMyAdmin", and `go`/`node` would match ordinary prose. It also does not say how a minimum version (FR-13) applies when the match came only from text, or whether a text-only match is labelled differently. *Fix:* define the match rule (e.g., case-insensitive whole-token on vendor/product or alias), and state that text-only matches are treated as "versions unknown" and labelled as such.
- **[high]** There is no precedence rule for conflicting values after a merge (§ FR-7, FR-9, FR-14, FR-18, FR-19) — FR-9 records the source of each value but never says which value *wins*. That choice drives the CVSS threshold (FR-14), the severity in the title (FR-18) and the "severity band rises" re-publish trigger (FR-19). EUVD, CERT-EU and CISA can disagree, or carry CVSS 3.1 and 4.0 side by side. *Fix:* state the precedence (e.g., highest available score, or CNA over ADP over others; CVSS 4.0 over 3.x) and use the same value in every FR that reads a score.
- **[high]** FR-19's "same GUID, published again as an update" may be undeliverable through Slack's RSS app (§ FR-17, FR-19) — RSS readers, including Slack's RSS integration as generally observed, de-duplicate on `guid` (or link). An item re-emitted with an unchanged GUID is then likely to be silently ignored, so the KEV/severity update never reaches the channel. The PRD treats this behaviour as given, without an `[ASSUMPTION]` tag. *Fix:* verify Slack's de-duplication key, and specify the update item's identity explicitly (e.g., a stable base GUID plus an update suffix such as `…#kev`, `…#critical`).
- **[medium]** FR-2b's "reference plus revision" identity conflicts with FR-19 (§ FR-2b, FR-19) — if each CERT-FR alerte revision is a distinct item, every revision re-posts. FR-19 says "Other metadata changes do not re-publish it", and the PRD does not say how a CERT-FR alerte merges with the EUVD record for the same CVE (FR-7). *Fix:* state whether a new alerte revision is a re-publish trigger (a third trigger in FR-19) or only de-duplication input.
- **[medium]** The PRD does not require state to survive restarts (§ FR-8, FR-16, FR-19; SM "Duplicates") — stable GUIDs, "publish again only when…", the 7-day backfill and the no-duplicates metric all depend on a persisted history of items and published states. A container restart that loses this state would re-post everything. *Fix:* add an NFR, e.g., "published-item state persists across container restarts and upgrades; a restart causes no re-posts."
- **[medium]** FR-5 has no done-condition (§ FR-5: "Add further sources, RSS or JSON, through configuration") — a generic RSS feed usually has no CVE ID, products or score, so the PRD does not say how FR-7–FR-9 and FR-12–FR-14 behave for such sources. Nor does it say what configuration has to express (field mapping? an ID regex?). *Fix:* define the minimum a configured source must provide (e.g., an ID extractor plus text) and what degrades (e.g., the item is matched on text only, with unknown score/versions).
- **[low]** Feed window size is unspecified (§ FR-17) — the PRD does not say how many items, or how many days, each feed carries. That matters for Slack polling gaps and for the 7-day backfill. *Fix:* state the window (e.g., the last N items or 14 days).

## Scope honesty — adequate
Out of Scope is strong. Each exclusion is specific and several carry a reason or a v2 marker ("candidate for v2", "they mostly repeat vendor bulletins already in EUVD"). Open-items density is very low (two Open Questions, no `[ASSUMPTION]` or `[NOTE FOR PM]` tags), which is fine for the stakes. Some inferences are nonetheless stated as fact and left unmarked.

### Findings
- **[low]** Unmarked assumptions (§ FR-17, FR-19; § Problem) — Slack RSS app behaviour (de-duplication, polling cadence) and "roughly 10% … relevant" (an estimate in the addendum) are presented as facts. *Fix:* tag them `[ASSUMPTION: …]`; the Slack one in particular (see the FR-19 finding).
- **[low]** "Optionally" in FR-2b is ambiguous (§ FR-2b) — it could mean a per-deployment configuration toggle or a v1 scope item that might slip. *Fix:* say "configurable per deployment, off by default", or move it to Out of Scope / v2.
- **[low]** Vendor bulletins are left unaddressed (§ Out of Scope; addendum "Wanted but missing: CERT-EU, vendor bulletins, KEV") — CERT-EU and KEV are covered, but vendor bulletins are neither in scope nor explicitly out, apart from being reachable through FR-5. *Fix:* add one line saying they are either via FR-5 configured sources or out for v1.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is short and probably feeds architecture and stories directly, so this dimension is judged lightly. FR IDs are unique and grouped by feature, cross-references resolve (FR-19 is referenced from SM "Duplicates"), and overflow detail correctly lives in the addendum. There are no UJs, which is appropriate here. The main gap is vocabulary: the unit of identity is the crux of FR-7, FR-8 and FR-19, yet it is named five ways.

### Findings
- **[medium]** There is no glossary, and the core nouns drift (§ FR-1, FR-7, FR-13, FR-18, FR-19; SM) — "record" (FR-1, FR-7), "advisory" (FR-2, FR-8), "alert" (FR-13–FR-15), "item" (FR-8, FR-18, SM) and "vulnerability" (FR-7, FR-19) are used interchangeably. "Endpoint" and "feed" are also used interchangeably. FR-7 merges *records* into a *vulnerability*, and FR-19 assigns the GUID to a *vulnerability*, but FR-8 identifies an *item*. The PRD never says whether these are the same unit. *Fix:* add a five-line glossary (source record → vulnerability → feed item; endpoint = feed) and align the FRs to it.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape is right: a capability spec for a single-operator tool, two lean user roles, no UJ theater, operational SMs, about two pages, and depth pushed to the addendum (API quirks, field lists, comparable tools). Rigor is light where it should be and substance stays high.

### Findings
- **[low]** Implementation prescriptions sit in the PRD body (§ NFR "Development"; FR-21 "provider interface") — "all development happens inside a container" is a team process constraint, not a product requirement. "Provider interface" prescribes design. Both are legitimate user-stated constraints, but they read as requirements on the product. *Fix:* group them under a short "Constraints (user-stated)" heading, or leave them only in the addendum's Technical preferences.

## Mechanical notes
- **ID continuity:** `FR-2b` breaks the otherwise contiguous FR-1…FR-23 sequence. That is harmless now, but story tooling that expects integer IDs may trip on it. Consider renumbering. NFRs and SMs have no IDs, so they cannot be cited from stories. Consider `NFR-1…` and `SM-1…`.
- **Cross-reference drift:** addendum § EUVD "Stability" says "Hence the source-adapter and contract-test requirement", but the PRD's Source resilience NFR mentions adapters and not contract tests. Either add contract tests to the NFR or drop the phrase from the addendum.
- **Assumptions Index:** n/a. There are no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, so there is nothing to round-trip (see the Scope honesty finding).
- **UJ protagonists:** n/a. There are no UJs, which suits this shape.
- **Required sections:** all present for hobby/solo stakes (Summary, Problem, Users, FRs, NFRs, SMs with counter-metric, Out of Scope, Open Questions). Frontmatter `status: draft` is consistent.
- **Terminology:** *alerte* / *avis* are italicised and used consistently. "EU KEV" (FR-3) maps cleanly to the addendum's `eukev_kev`.
